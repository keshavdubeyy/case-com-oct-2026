# Lab-to-Episode Linkage Diagnostics

**Status: diagnostics only. The assignment algorithm in `scripts/build_processed.py::build_lab_journey()` was NOT changed.** This document quantifies the ambiguity risk flagged but left unmeasured in `VALIDATION_REPORT.md` Part 6 / Part 13 (P1): the greedy, chronological-episode-order, nearest-date assignment of `lab_tests.csv` rows to episodes could, in principle, misassign a lab record between two closely-spaced teleconsultations for the same patient. This document measures how often that's actually possible and how often it actually happened, using the current data — it does not fix it.

Generated by: `scripts/lab_linkage_diagnostics.py` → `processed/lab_linkage_diagnostics.json` / `public/data/lab_linkage_diagnostics.json`. Surfaced in-app on the **Data Quality & Record Linkage** page.

## Method

The production algorithm (unchanged, reproduced exactly for verification — see "Reproduction check" below):

1. Resolve each `lab_tests.csv` row to a canonical patient via row-level entity linkage (same scoring as every other source table).
2. For each test-advised episode (`test_advised='Yes'`, patient link resolved), sorted by ascending `consult_date`, greedily claim the *closest unused* lab record of the same patient whose `order_date` falls in `[consult_date - 3 days, consult_date + 45 days]`.
3. Each lab record can be claimed by at most one episode (`used[]` flag), enforced by construction.

This diagnostic script re-derives the same row-level linkage and the same greedy assignment, in the same order, purely to retain `lab_record_id` through the process (the production pipeline doesn't keep it downstream of `episode_fact.csv`, since the episode fact table stores the *lab record's fields*, not its ID) — so contested records can be traced back to which episode won them.

**Reproduction check:** the diagnostic's re-derived matched count is **1,944 / 1,944**, exactly matching the production pipeline's `lab_link_status='matched'` count. This confirms the diagnostic is measuring the actual deployed algorithm, not an approximation of it.

## Findings

| Diagnostic | Count | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patients with ≥2 test-advised episodes whose `[-3,+45]`-day windows overlap each other | 99 | — |
| Overlapping episode pairs (structural precondition for ambiguity) | 106 | — |
| Lab records with a resolved patient link | 1,951 | — |
| Lab records eligible for **more than one** episode (within window) | 89 | 4.56% of resolved lab records |
| Episodes competing for at least one contested lab record | 171 | 3.10% of all 5,516 episodes |
| **Matched assignments that are order-sensitive** (the lab record actually assigned was also eligible for ≥1 other episode of the same patient) | **89 of 1,944** | **4.58%** |

### Interpretation

- **This is a real, non-zero ambiguity**, not a theoretical concern: 89 of the 1,944 lab-to-episode assignments the pipeline actually made (4.58%) involve a lab record that a different episode-processing order could plausibly have assigned elsewhere. This can happen when the same patient has two teleconsultations close enough together (a repeat visit, a referral follow-up) that their `[-3,+45]`-day windows overlap and a single lab order falls in the overlap.
- **It is not large enough, on the evidence gathered here, to justify changing the algorithm without further design work.** 4.58% of matched assignments (89 records) is a modest, bounded exposure — it does not call into question the aggregate metrics (test-advised counts, overall completion rate, median timing) at the dashboard level, since even a "wrong" assignment still goes to *some* real episode of the same real patient with a plausible order_date, not to an unrelated patient or a fabricated one. The risk is concentrated in **episode-level** attribution (e.g., "did *this specific* follow-up episode's lab result come back," for 171 episodes), not in population-level rates.
- **No lab record was ever assigned to more than one episode** — the `used[]` one-time-use rule holds by construction, verified again here.

## Recommended next step (not implemented in this pass)

If episode-level lab attribution for closely-spaced repeat consultations becomes analytically important (e.g., feeding a per-episode model feature rather than an aggregate dashboard rate), replace the greedy per-episode assignment with a **global optimal assignment** (e.g., minimum-total-|delta-days| bipartite matching between all eligible (episode, lab-record) pairs for a contested patient, rather than resolving episodes one at a time in date order). This would remove the order-sensitivity entirely for the ~171 affected episodes without changing the matched/unmatched totals materially. Not implemented here per the instruction to diagnose, not silently fix, and because the measured exposure (4.6%) did not clear a "materially significant" bar on its own.
